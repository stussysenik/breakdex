Here is a new Spec‑Kit–style specification that removes the playhead, uses two easy‑to‑grab handles in Apple blue, and reallocates vertical space to the bottom action row while avoiding unnecessary animations. [1][2]

## Specify

- Intent: deliver a minimal, two‑point trimmer that emphasizes precision through large touch targets, Apple system blue styling, and a layout that gives more space to the bottom buttons than the gap between preview and timeline. [1][2]
- Non‑negotiables: no playhead, no thumbnail strip, no decorative animations; the preview is the primary focus and the control is a single line with two handles. [1][2]
- Outcome: start and end times are emitted for export, with live preview scrubbing while dragging but without animated transitions. [3][1]

## Experience

- Visual hierarchy: video preview on top, compact timeline immediately below, time readouts under the timeline, and a prominent bottom action row with increased spacing and clear affordances. [2][4]
- Controls: timeline shows an inactive track outside the range and an active in‑range segment in systemBlue with two circular handles that appear slim but expose large interactive envelopes. [2][4]
- Styling: handles and the active segment use systemBlue; labels and inactive segments use secondary colors to keep the chrome deferential to content. [2][4]

## Layout and spacing

- Grid: adopt an 8‑pt system with these vertical tokens—Preview→Timeline: 12 pt, Timeline→Readouts: 8 pt, Readouts→Actions: 40 pt, Actions→Safe‑Area: 12 pt; horizontal padding: 20–24 pt depending on size class. [2][4]
- Bottom actions: allocate more vertical space to “Back,” “Save Trim,” and one contextual action than the space between preview and timeline to increase target clarity and reduce accidental taps. [2][4]
- Consistency: maintain spacing tokens across orientations and size classes, and ensure intra‑button spacing ≥24 pt for comfortable separation. [2][4]

## Interaction model

- Drag only: the two handles are the sole interactive elements; dragging left/right sets start/end, and the preview seeks live to the handle’s time without any animated easing. [5][6]
- Precision zoom (optional toggle): pinch on the line increases temporal resolution 2×–4× only while the gesture is active; if disabled, the trimmer still guarantees frame‑accurate snapping. [7][6]
- Feedback: brief haptics on grab and release and light ticks at frame boundaries convey precision without visual motion. [7][4]

## Functional requirements

- FR1 Two‑handle constraint: start cannot pass end and end cannot pass start; minimum span equals one frame at the asset’s time base. [3][4]
- FR2 Range highlight: the in‑range segment renders in systemBlue and the out‑of‑range segments render in secondary color with sufficient contrast in both light and dark modes. [2][4]
- FR3 Live preview: while dragging either handle, the preview displays the exact frame at that time with coalesced updates to preserve responsiveness. [5][4]
- FR4 Readouts: persistent Start, Duration, and End labels update on every drag tick; transient badges appear above the active handle during drag and vanish on release. [2][4]
- FR5 Save enablement: “Save Trim” is disabled until the current range differs from the full duration, then enabled immediately with no animation. [1][4]

## Non‑functional requirements

- Touch comfort: each handle must expose a minimum 44×44 pt interactive area via contentShape, regardless of its visual size. [2][4]
- Performance: UI updates target 60 fps with live scrubbing latency kept low by throttled seeks and avoiding implicit animations on drag‑mutated state. [6][8]
- Accessibility: semantic labels like “Start handle at t=…” and “End handle at t=…” are announced, and contrast meets platform guidance. [2][4]

## Contracts

- Inputs: asset reference, duration, time scale/frame rate, initial start/end, and configuration flags for snapping and optional precision zoom. [3][4]
- Outputs: startTime and endTime as precise values suitable for building CMTimeRange in the downstream exporter. [3][4]
- Events: onBeginDrag(handle), onChange(handle,time), onEndDrag(handle,time), and onConfirm to coordinate with the host screen and analytics. [1][4]

## Animation policy

- Prohibition: no implicit or global .animation modifiers on values mutated by gestures to prevent “Invalid sample AnimatablePair… time > last time” logs and jitter. [8][5]
- Allowed: optional explicit animation for transient badge fade in/out only, not for handle position, scale, or range highlight. [6][4]

## Acceptance criteria

- AC‑A Layout: spacing matches tokens with greater distance before the bottom action row than between preview and timeline across compact/regular classes. [2][4]
- AC‑B Precision: dragging shows frame‑accurate boundaries; the minimum selectable span is one frame, and snapping occurs deterministically at the asset’s time base. [3][4]
- AC‑C Comfort: both handles are comfortably grabbable on first attempt due to ≥44 pt targets, verified in accessibility audit. [2][4]
- AC‑D Stability: no “Invalid sample AnimatablePair…” logs during rapid drags or edge‑proximity interactions. [8][4]
- AC‑E Export parity: exported clip boundaries match previewed start/end exactly when consumed by the exporter. [3][4]

## Tasks

- T1 Apply spacing tokens and re‑layout: Preview→Timeline 12 pt, Timeline→Readouts 8 pt, Readouts→Actions 40 pt, Actions→Safe‑Area 12 pt; horizontal 20–24 pt padding. [2][4]
- T2 Implement two‑handle track with active systemBlue segment and secondary inactive segments, honoring dark/light modes. [2][4]
- T3 Enforce 44×44 pt interactive envelopes around handles and verify with debug overlays during QA. [2][4]
- T4 Wire gesture pipeline to update preview on drag ticks with no implicit animation and with coalesced updates for responsiveness. [5][6]
- T5 Add persistent readouts and transient drag badges; ensure badges appear only while dragging and do not animate handle motion. [2][4]
- T6 Gate Save enablement on range difference and add unit/UI tests for edge conditions including one‑frame span and near‑overlap. [1][4]
- T7 Run stress tests to confirm absence of AnimatablePair errors and verify export parity on constant and variable frame‑rate assets. [8][3]

## Risks and mitigations

- Perceived stutter on large files: throttle seeks using a display‑link cadence and defer non‑essential updates during drag. [6][4]
- Variable FPS assets: compute snapping and minimum span from the media time base rather than assuming constant frame rate. [3][4]
- Over‑minimal cues: rely on transient badges and haptics to maintain clarity without reintroducing a playhead or thumbnails. [7][4]

## Review checklist

- Spacing tokens verified on all target devices and orientations with larger gap before actions than between preview and timeline. [2][4]
- Two handles only, systemBlue in‑range, and secondary out‑of‑range rendering validated in light/dark modes. [2][4]
- No implicit animations on drag‑mutated values; console is free of AnimatablePair warnings under rapid thrash tests. [8][5]
- Exported range matches preview boundaries across sample assets, including one‑frame trims. [3][4]
