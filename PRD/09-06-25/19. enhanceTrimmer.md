The extra blue past the dots and the “invalid range” come from two root causes: the geometry is computed against the full view instead of the “usable track span” (so the fill overshoots the handle centers), and the range math clamps in continuous space instead of frame‑snapped CMTime with a hard one‑frame gap, letting the endpoints collide numerically. [1][2][3][4]
Fix both by defining a canonical track coordinate system with insets equal to handle radius, mapping positions to time through frame‑snapped CMTime, and enforcing a single‑frame minimum gap before painting the active segment while decoupling finger tracking from preview seeking. [5][3][6][7]

## Root causes
- Track/fill overflow: the selected segment is sized from raw view width and offset by handle visual width, so the active blue extends beyond thumb centers at the edges and during zoom, a common pitfall in custom two‑thumb sliders and custom track imagery. [1][2][5]
- Invalid range: start/end are clamped in float seconds, then rounded visually, allowing sub‑frame overlap or rounding inversion; the fix is to quantize to the asset’s time base using CMTime and enforce a one‑frame gap invariant on those snapped values. [3][4]
- Perceived lag: the handle position is tied to player seeks; the UI must lead and the preview must trail via a single‑flight, coalesced seek scheduler to avoid dots “waiting” on I/O. [6][8]

## Canonical geometry (no overshoot, no gaps)
- Define an inset $$r$$ equal to the visual handle radius; the usable track span is $$W_u = W - 2r$$, where $$W$$ is the control’s content width inside horizontal padding. [2][9]
- Normalize both handles to percentages $$s \in [10]$$ and $$e \in [10]$$ with the invariant $$e \ge s + \delta$$, and compute handle centers as $$x_s = r + s \cdot W_u$$ and $$x_e = r + e \cdot W_u$$. [1][9]
- Paint the active segment from $$x_s$$ to $$x_e$$ exactly; because endpoints are defined at handle centers, there is never “extra blue” beyond the dots even when scaling or zooming. [2][5]

## Time mapping and range invariants (frame‑exact)
- Map position to raw seconds $$t_r = \frac{x - r}{W_u} \cdot D$$, then quantize to the nearest frame $$t_q = \text{round}\!\left(\frac{t_r}{\Delta}\right)\cdot \Delta$$ where $$\Delta = 1/\text{fps}$$ or the effective frame duration from the asset. [3][4]
- Represent all internal times as CMTime with preferred timescale ≥600 (or fps×10) to minimize cumulative rounding, and never store float seconds as the source of truth. [3][4]
- Enforce the invariant $$t_{end} \ge t_{start} + \Delta$$ after snapping, clamping proposals with $$t_{start} = \max(0, \min(t_{start}, t_{end} - \Delta))$$ and $$t_{end} = \min(D, \max(t_{end}, t_{start} + \Delta))$$. [3][4]

## Input and preview pipelines (no catch‑up lag)
- Input loop: track the finger at display rate using coalesced/predicted touches from a pan‑recognizer bridge, update $$x_s$$ or $$x_e$$ every sample, and re‑render immediately; never tie handle motion to player state. [7][11]
- Preview loop: maintain a single in‑flight seek; store latestTargetTime and issue the next seek only on completion with appropriate tolerances, eliminating overlapping seeks and thrash. [6][8]
- Fallback: if mean seek latency during drag exceeds a threshold, switch to frame stepping or AVAssetImageGenerator with tight requestedTimeTolerance to keep preview responsiveness. [8][12][13]

## Interaction precision and feedback
- Continuous precision: modulate sensitivity by velocity and vertical offset so fast drags traverse quickly and slow/offset drags resolve into single‑frame steps without a separate mode. [14][15]
- Haptic detents: trigger a light tick exactly once per snapped frame index change and soft impacts on grab/release to confirm precision without visual animation. [16][17]
- No implicit animations: remove all implicit/global animations on drag‑mutated values to prevent “invalid animatable sample” warnings and visual stutter. [17][18]

## Visual and accessibility rules
- Paint out‑of‑range in secondary and in‑range in systemBlue; place two circular handles with a white stroke on thin track, and render actions directly on true black with native button styles. [19][20]
- Each handle exposes ≥44×44 pt interactive area via contentShape and VoiceOver labels “Start at t=…” and “End at t=…”, matching platform HIG. [19]

## Definitive acceptance tests
- Geometry: at $$s=0$$ and $$e=1$$, the active segment begins and ends exactly at handle centers with zero blue overshoot on both edges across all widths and zoom scales. [2][5]
- Range: on any drag sequence, the snapped times always satisfy $$t_{end} \ge t_{start} + \Delta$$; attempts to collide clamp cleanly with no visual jitter or state oscillation. [3][4]
- Tracking: under rapid reversals, the handle remains under the finger while preview updates trail smoothly via the scheduler with no overlapping seeks or dropped completions. [7][6]
- Export parity: creating a CMTimeRange from snapped start/end yields an output whose first and last frames match the previewed boundaries on CFR and VFR assets. [3][4]
