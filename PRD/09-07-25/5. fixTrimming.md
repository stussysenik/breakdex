## 1. **Handle Alignment and Hit Target Consistency**

- **Issue:** The handle centers do not perfectly sit on the timeline ends, and touch areas can feel misaligned.
- **Solution:**  
  - Unify the handle width—declare a single constant (e.g., `let handleWidth: CGFloat = 44`) and only use this everywhere: for the handle’s frame, hit area, and offset math.
  - The timeline’s drawable (active) region must be inset by exactly half `handleWidth` on each end.  
  - When the time equals start or end, the handle’s center must sit flush with the very start or end of the blue range, never overhang or undershoot.  
  - Redraw/prototype this geometry in isolation and refactor any “correction offsets” until the visual and interactive logic both use the same number.

## 2. **Handle Visual and Feedback Refinement**

- **Issue:** Handles look like vertical white bars; they lack a sense of affordance and “touchable-ness,” and don’t animate or communicate engagement.
- **Solution:**  
  - Replace the handle with a `RoundedRectangle` (width: 4, height: e.g., 36–44, small corner radius).  
  - On drag, gently animate the handle’s height and shadow (height +8, shadow +1) so it appears “picked up.”  
  - Center the handle vertically and “grow” the blue bar underneath it only between handle centers.  
  - Subtly raise the active handle’s zIndex to ensure it’s never occluded near edges.
  - Keep the fill solid white or with a slight overlay for press, and eliminate any cap or decorative element—it should look intentional, light, and direct.

***

## 3. **Precision Drag Physics with Guardrails**

- **Issue:** As handles near each other, drag can become jumpy or slip past the one-frame gap too easily, losing fine control.
- **Solution:**  
  - Calculate pixel distance between handles and dynamically scale a friction/resistance factor (`frictionFactor`) as this distance decreases (e.g., using an exponential or piecewise curve).
  - In the drag gesture’s update, multiply `dragValue.translation.width` by the `frictionFactor`—so moves are smooth and nimble at wide separations but “slow down” as the handles approach minimum distance.
  - Clamp all updates after snapping to the one-frame gap CMTime logic, so handles can never mathematically overlap.
  - Optionally add haptic “ticks” at every frame change in this tight region for even firmer micro-adjustment feedback.

## 4. **Final Quality Pass: Minimal and Confident Visuals**

- Ensure the track itself never accepts touches—use `.allowsHitTesting(false)` and `.contentShape(Rectangle())` only on the handle view itself.
- Time labels should remain monospaced, centered, and spaced at least 8pt off the handle body for clarity at all times.
- Buttons should be visually balanced and maintain at least 24pt horizontal separation for accessibility.
- Run visual tests at both dark and light system appearances and at all scaling settings to confirm alignment, visibility, and tactile feedback.
