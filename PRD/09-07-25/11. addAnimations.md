Here is a concise Spec‑Kit–style “final polish” plan that keeps the app minimal, fluid, and precise, with motion used only to clarify action and confirm success. [1][2]
Core interactions stay UIKit‑driven for touch fidelity and frame‑exact trimming, while SwiftUI composes screens and timecode displays. [3][4]

## Intent
- Deliver a calm, minimal UI with purposeful micro‑interactions so navigation feels continuous, edits feel precise, and saves feel like a firm, confident confirmation. [1][5]
- Retain UIKit for high‑fidelity gestures and coalesced touches in the trimmer, and use SwiftUI for layout, lists, and labels. [4][3]

## Scope
- Finalize motion, haptics, and styling for navigation, “Select a clip,” create/save combo, list view selection, review buttons, and trimmer timecode readouts. [1][6]
- Guarantee accessibility, reduce motion alternatives, and performance hygiene across all micro‑interactions. [2][1]

## Motion principles
- Use motion to explain spatial relationships and confirm results, not to decorate, and mirror transitions on enter/exit so directionality is obvious. [1][5]
- Keep timings tight and consistent: most micro‑interactions 120–300 ms with gentle ease‑out/ease‑in‑out, and provide fade substitutions under Reduce Motion. [1][7]

## Navigation
- Push/pop: 220 ms slide with ease‑in‑out; if the source cell has a title/thumbnail, use a restrained shared‑element morph to the detail header, mirrored on pop. [1][7]
- Reduce Motion: replace slide+shared element with a cross‑fade at the same duration. [1][2]

## Select a clip
- Button press: scale 1.0→0.97→1.0 in 160 ms ease‑out with soft haptic to acknowledge activation without stealing focus. [8][7]
- Success: fade in timecode row in 180 ms and pulse the button opacity/scale subtly for confirmation. [1][5]

## Create combo
- Present sheet: translate up ~16–20 pt and fade 0→1 in ~220 ms with a light spring (high damping) for a responsive, non‑bouncy feel. [1][7]
- Field focus: add a tiny lift (+2 pt shadow) on focus and restore on blur to signal edit mode. [1][5]

## Save name (“firm handshake”)
- On tap: fill animation to 100% in ~180 ms, label cross‑fades to a check for 120 ms, and a medium impact haptic fires, then settle 1.02→1.0. [8][7]
- Toast: non‑modal “Saved” toast fades in/out 200/200 ms away from the primary task area to keep flow. [1][5]

## List selection
- Cell tap: 120 ms 1.0→1.01 scale and subtle elevation, then shared‑element carryover of title/thumbnail into detail. [1][7]
- Detail arrival: stagger secondary elements (meta, controls) with 30–40 ms offsets and 120–160 ms fades. [1][5]

## Review buttons (gamified)
- Press: 1.0→0.96 press scale with soft haptic, release to 1.0 plus a brief color flash tied to outcome in ~160 ms. [8][7]
- Progress: animate streak/progress fill in 180 ms ease‑out; for milestones, emit a tiny sparkle hint (opacity/scale dots), not full confetti. [1][5]

## Trimmer polish
- Handles: thin 4 pt bars centered on the track with ≥44×44 pt hit areas; track is non‑interactive. [6][9]
- Input: UIPanGestureRecognizer with coalesced touches for display‑rate tracking; position updates are unanimated for accuracy; scale/shadow only on grab/release. [4][1]
- Snapping: map x→time, snap to nearest frame, enforce one‑frame gap, and render active segment center‑to‑center with no overhang. [6][1]
- Seeking: single‑flight seek queue with coalesced latest target and tight tolerances; fall back to step/image generation if latency spikes. [10][11]
- Timecodes: Start/Duration/End monospaced labels update per snapped CMTime; fade in on load over ~160 ms. [6][1]

## Accessibility
- Touch targets ≥44 pt, VoiceOver labels like “Start handle, at 00:03.91,” and full Reduce Motion parity via fades. [9][1]
- Ensure contrast on dark backgrounds and haptics are complementary, not the sole signal. [2][8]

## Performance guardrails
- Keep all micro‑interactions under ~300 ms, avoid chained animations that block input, and profile seeks to prevent overlap. [1][10]
- Prefer UIKit animators for interruptible transitions and bridge into SwiftUI via UIViewRepresentable where needed. [12][3]

## Acceptance criteria
- Navigation and sheets animate within specified durations with mirrored reversals and Reduce Motion fallbacks. [1][2]
- Select/Create/Save flows give immediate visual + haptic feedback without blocking the next action. [8][1]
- Review buttons feel game‑like but restrained, updating streak/progress smoothly. [7][5]
- Trimmer handles remain under the finger with coalesced touches, never cross due to one‑frame gap, and timecodes match exported boundaries. [4][6]

## Implementation tasks
- Build a MotionCatalog with named durations/curves (e.g., tap, push, save, listEnter) reused across modules. [1][5]
- Wire UIKit animators for navigation/sheets and expose convenience wrappers to SwiftUI where composition is needed. [12][3]
- Integrate haptic helpers: soft/medium/selection detents mapped to tap, save, and trimmer frame steps. [8][1]
- Add Reduce Motion checks that swap slides for fades and disable parallax. [1][2]
- Instrument the seek queue and animation durations with lightweight logs for QA thresholds. [10][11]

This spec keeps motion minimal and meaningful, ensures tactile certainty at key moments, and preserves precise trimming by leaning on UIKit’s high‑fidelity input and SwiftUI’s clear composition. [1][3]
