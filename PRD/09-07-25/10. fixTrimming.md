## Architecture
- Container: a custom UIView “TrimmerView” that owns one CALayer for the gray track, one CALayer for the blue in‑range segment, and two UIView handles for start and end. [2]
- SwiftUI bridge: expose TrimmerView through UIViewRepresentable so layout and labels remain SwiftUI while input/rendering stays UIKit. [1]
- Data flow: the view holds normalized positions s,e∈[4], publishes snapped CMTime start/end via bindings, and accepts duration/fps inputs from the view model. [1][2]

## Interaction model
- Handles: 4 pt visual width bars (rounded rect) at handle centers, each inside a 44×44 pt invisible hit area to satisfy touch comfort; track is non‑interactive. [2][5]
- Input: attach a UIPanGestureRecognizer to each handle and read coalesced touches every frame for high‑fidelity finger tracking; update handle position immediately on each coalesced sample. [3][2]
- Precision: map x→time, snap to nearest frame, and enforce end ≥ start + one‑frame gap after snapping; blue segment renders strictly from center‑to‑center to avoid any “extra blue.” [2]

## Timecode display

- Below track: Start, Duration, End monospaced labels that update on every drag tick from the snapped CMTime values, matching export parity. [2][1]

## Seeking strategy
- Single‑flight seek queue: coalesce the latest target during drags and issue the next seek only in the prior completion to avoid overlapping seeks and jitter. [7][8]
- Tolerances: use tight or zero tolerance when frame‑stepping is supported and relax only when needed to maintain responsiveness on heavier assets. [7][8]

## Visual details

## Acceptance criteria
- Geometry: bar handles align exactly to the blue segment endpoints across zoom/widths with no overshoot at 0% and 100%. [2][1]
- Precision: dragging any handle with rapid reversals keeps the bar under the finger due to coalesced touches, while Start/Duration/End remain frame‑snapped and stable. [3][2]
- Stability: seek queue emits no overlapping requests during drags and preview updates feel smooth on device. [7][8]

## Implementation tasks
- Build TrimmerView (UIKit): layers for track/active range + two handle subviews with 44×44 pt hit areas and 4 pt visual bars centered inside. [2]
- Wire gestures: add separate UIPanGestureRecognizer to each handle and consume UITouch coalesced samples to update normalized s,e immediately. [3]
- Quantization: convert normalized positions to time, snap to nearest frame, enforce one‑frame gap, and publish CMTime start/end to the SwiftUI model. [2][1]
- Bridge: wrap TrimmerView in UIViewRepresentable and bind inputs/outputs (duration, fps, start/end) to SwiftUI for the labels and Save button logic. [1]
- Seek scheduler: implement single‑flight seek with coalescing and tolerance policy for smooth preview while dragging. [7][8]

## Why UIKit here
- UIKit gives direct control of pan recognizers and coalesced/predicted touches for display‑rate fidelity, which is ideal for a precise, low‑latency trimming control. [3][1]
- SwiftUI remains for composition and labels, but the high‑frequency input and rendering are handled imperatively where the platform APIs are most mature. [1][2]
