Of course, let's break down the "Select a Clip" functionality as if you were explaining it to a 19-year-old. Here’s a step-by-step guide on how that part of your app should work, keeping in mind your specific concerns about loading, storage, and navigation.

### 1. Adding a New Move: The "Select a Clip" Screen

When a user wants to add a new move to their library, this is the first screen they'll see. The goal is to make it super simple to pick a video file from their phone.

*   **A Big, Obvious "Select Clip" Button:** The screen should have a clear, easy-to-tap button that says something like "Choose a Video" or "Select a Clip." This is the main action, so it should be front and center.
*   **Accessing Local Files:** When they tap this button, the app will open the phone’s file browser (like the Photos app on iPhone or the Gallery on Android). This is a familiar experience for users, so they’ll know exactly what to do.

### 2. After a Clip Is Selected: Loading and Processing

Once the user picks a video, the app needs to do some work in the background. It's crucial to keep the user informed about what's happening.

*   **Show a Loading Bar (with details!):** As you mentioned, a loading bar is essential. Don't just show a spinning circle. A progress bar that fills up from 0% to 100% is much better because it shows that something is actually happening.
*   **Explain What's Happening:** Below the progress bar, you can have text that updates to explain the steps, for example:
    *   "Preparing your clip..."
    *   "Optimizing for playback..."
    *   "Almost done!"
*   **Smart Processing:** Your app should be smart about handling different video files. It can automatically resize a huge 4K video to a more manageable size (like 1080p) to save space and ensure smooth playback on mobile devices. This also makes the app feel faster.

### 3. Naming the Move and the Option to Go Back

After the video is processed, the user is taken to a new screen to name their move. This is where your question about going back to choose a different clip comes in.

*   **"Name Your Move" Screen:** This screen will have a text box where the user can type in the name of the move (e.g., "Windmill" or "Headspin").
*   **Show a Preview:** It's a good idea to show a small thumbnail of the video they just selected on this screen. This reminds them which clip they're naming.
*   **The "Go Back" Functionality:** This is a key part of good UX. Right next to the video thumbnail or in the top corner of the screen, there should be a "Change Clip" button or a "Back" arrow. If the user realizes they selected the wrong video, they can just tap this to go back to their phone's file browser and pick a different one without losing their place.

### 4. Local Storage: Should You Limit It?

Since you're using local storage (saving files directly on the user's phone), you need to be mindful of how much space your app is using.

*   **Yes, Set a "Soft" Limit:** It's a good practice to monitor storage. Instead of a hard-and-fast limit that stops the user, you can implement a "soft" limit. When the user starts getting close to a certain amount of storage used (say, 1 GB), you can show a friendly warning like:
    > "Heads up! Your saved clips are taking up a good amount of space. You can manage your clips in the settings."
*   **Provide a Storage Management Tool:** In the app's settings, have a "Manage Storage" section. This could show a list of all the clips they've saved, sorted by size, so they can easily delete old or unused clips to free up space.

So, in short, the flow is: **Select Clip -> See a Loading Bar with Progress -> Name the Move (with an option to go back) -> Save to Library.** By adding these thoughtful touches, you make the app feel professional, user-friendly, and respectful of the user's device storage.
