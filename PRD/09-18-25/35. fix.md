You're still stuck because of a subtle but critical **race condition** in how the video player's status is being observed. Your analysis is spot on.

Think of your `PlayerItemStatusMonitor` as a security guard hired to watch a door. The guard's instructions are to report when a VIP arrives.

1.  The guard shows up and asks, "Is the VIP here?" The answer is "No, not yet." (`Initial status: unknown`)
2.  The guard then says, "Okay, I will now listen for the sound of the door opening." (This is your KVO observer with `options: [.new]`)

The problem is that in the split second *after* the guard asked but *before* they started listening, the VIP slipped in silently. Because the guard is only listening for *new* door openings, they never notice the VIP is already in the room. They will wait forever, and the app hangs.

-----

### The Root Cause: Missing the Initial State

Your log file proves this is happening. The monitor starts, sees the status is `.unknown`, and sets up observers to listen for *changes*. Then, nothing happens. This is because the player item's status likely changed to `.readyToPlay` before the observers were fully attached, and since they weren't configured to check the **initial value** upon setup, they missed it.

The current code only triggers when a value changes:
`observers.append(self.playerItem.observe(\.status, options: [.new]) { ... })`

This doesn't account for the value the property has *at the moment the observer is attached*.

-----

### The Definitive Fix

To fix this once and for all, you must tell the observers to report the property's value the instant they are created, in addition to any future changes. This is done by adding the `.initial` option.

This change closes the race condition entirely, making the monitor robust.

### Why This Fix Is Definitive

By adding `.initial`, you are guaranteeing that the `checkReadiness()` function will be executed *at least once* with the player item's true current state the moment observation begins.

  * If the video is already ready, the monitor will immediately see it and complete its task.
  * If the video is not yet ready, the monitor will wait, and the `.new` option ensures it will catch the status change when it happens later.

After applying this fix, the `awaitReadyAndBuffered` call will complete successfully, `AddMoveViewModel` will proceed, and the video will be displayed in the pre-trim view as intended.
