### In-Depth Analysis: The Root Cause

The problem is that the `AVPlayerViewRepresentable` is being given **two conflicting sources of truth** for the `AVPlayer`:

1.  A direct `player: AVPlayer` property passed into its `init`.
2.  A `viewModel: UnifiedVideoPlayerViewModel` property, which *also* contains an `avPlayer`.

During SwiftUI's rapid view re-rendering cycles, the `player` passed into the `init` becomes stale. The `updateUIView` method then contains confusing logic trying to reconcile these two sources, leading to a state where the `AVPlayerLayer` ends up with a `nil` player. This affects **both the trimming and naming views** because they both use this same flawed component.

**The First-Principles Failure:** A component must have one, and only one, source of truth. The `UnifiedVideoPlayerViewModel` is designed to be this persistent source of truth. By passing in a separate, direct `player` reference, we created an ambiguity that breaks the view's lifecycle.

  - **The Symptom:** The final error, `⚠️ Tap received but no player assigned`, shows that despite the attempt to fix it, the `PlayerView`'s layer ends up disconnected from the player, making it inert.
