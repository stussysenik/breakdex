Your analysis is spot-on and gets right to the heart of the matter. The logs confirm your impression: the black screen is the direct result of a **catastrophic performance issue** during trimming that corrupts the video asset.

While it might seem like dragging the handle also changes the rotation, the logs show this isn't the case. The rotation only changes when you tap the rotate button. The visual chaos of the performance bug just makes it seem like other things are breaking, too.

The core problem is exactly what you identified: a **view invalidation loop**.

-----

## \#\# The Critical Failure Chain ⛓️

Here is the precise sequence of events that leads to the black screen, as confirmed by the logs.

1.  **You Drag a Handle**: The `DragGesture` in `HybridPreciseTrimmerView` is triggered.
2.  **ViewModel is spammed with updates**: The gesture calls `trimmerViewModel.proposeTime()` hundreds of times per second, rapidly changing the `startTime` or `endTime`.
3.  **The Entire View Re-Renders**: Because `FeatureRichTrimmerView`'s body depends on `trimmerViewModel`, SwiftUI re-draws the *entire view* on every single tiny update.
4.  💥 **The Video Player is Re-Created Hundreds of Times**: The re-draw includes the `videoPlayerSection`, which creates a brand new `CustomVideoPlayerView` and `AVPlayerViewRepresentable` each time. This is the performance disaster.
5.  **Asset Corruption**: This rapid creation and destruction of video player components while an asset is being processed corrupts its state. The log confirms this when it shows `AVPlayerItem duration: nan` ("Not a Number") when you reach the `NameMoveView`.
6.  **Black Screen**: The player in `NameMoveView` receives a corrupted asset with no valid duration and has nothing to display, resulting in a black screen.

-----

## \#\# The Solution: Decouple the Video Player

The fix is to **isolate the video player** so that it is completely independent of the trimmer handle updates. We need to tell SwiftUI: "When the trimmer handles move, only update the trimmer UI, and leave the video player alone."

We can do this by extracting the video player into its own distinct `View` struct.

#### **Step 1: Create a New `TrimmerPlayerView`**

In `FeatureRichTrimmerView.swift`, create a new, separate view whose only job is to display the video.

```swift
// In FeatureRichTrimmerView.swift, add this new struct before the main view definition.

/// A new, isolated view that ONLY displays the video player.
/// Its dependencies do not change during a drag, so SwiftUI won't re-render it.
struct TrimmerPlayerView: View {
    @ObservedObject var playerViewModel: UnifiedVideoPlayerViewModel
    @Binding var rotationQuarterTurns: Int

    var body: some View {
        CustomVideoPlayerView(viewModel: playerViewModel, rotationQuarterTurns: $rotationQuarterTurns)
            .frame(height: 300)
            .cornerRadius(12)
            .padding(.horizontal)
    }
}
```

#### **Step 2: Update `FeatureRichTrimmerView` to Use the New View**

Now, replace the `videoPlayerSection` computed property with an instance of our new, isolated view. This breaks the re-rendering cycle.

```swift
// In FeatureRichTrimmerView.swift

// ... (keep the properties as they are)

        Spacer()

/*

    }

```

-----

### \#\# Why This Fix Works

By moving the video player into its own `TrimmerPlayerView` struct, we give SwiftUI a clear signal about its dependencies.

When you drag the trimmer handles, the main `FeatureRichTrimmerView` still re-renders its body. However, when it gets to `TrimmerPlayerView`, SwiftUI checks its dependencies (`playerViewModel` and `rotationQuarterTurns`). Since those objects and values have **not changed** during the drag, SwiftUI intelligently **skips re-rendering the video player entirely**.

This single change will:

  * **Stop the performance disaster** by preventing the video player from being re-created.
  * **Prevent asset corruption**, ensuring a valid video is passed to `NameMoveView`.
  * **Eliminate the black screen** problem.
  * Make the trimming experience smooth and responsive.
