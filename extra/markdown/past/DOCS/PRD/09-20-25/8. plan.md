## Key Issues and What's Missing ❌

The "DEEP ANALYSIS" section in your logs points to several critical implementation failures that are the root cause of your struggles.

### Architectural Flaw: State Object Churn

This is the central problem. Your app is repeatedly creating and destroying expensive objects instead of managing their lifecycle properly.

  * **Chronic View Re-computation:** The logs show that views like `TrimmerWrapper` and `PreTrimView` are being re-evaluated more than 15 times in quick succession. This is a direct result of state objects being recreated, which forces SwiftUI to rebuild the view hierarchy unnecessarily, causing performance drops.
  * **State Wrappers Re-Initialized:** The `ObservableWrapper` is constantly being initialized and de-initialized (`deinit`) as you move between screens. This confirms that the state is not being preserved.

### Dual State Management Systems

The logs reveal you have two competing systems for managing state:

1.  `AddMoveState` (an enum)
2.  `AddMoveAppState` (an `@Observable` class)

This creates confusion and a high risk of synchronization issues. The application lacks a **single source of truth**, making state management complex and error-prone.

### Player Lifecycle Mismanagement 🌪️

The video player is not being handled efficiently across view transitions.

  * **Player Instance Leaks:** The logs show `PlayerView` instance counts fluctuating from 1 to 0 and back to 1. This means the player is being destroyed when you navigate away from a view and recreated when you return, instead of being preserved and reused. This is highly inefficient.
  * **Monitoring Overhead:** The `VideoHealthMonitor` is started, stopped, and restarted multiple times. Its lifecycle should be tied to the video asset itself, not the view's appearance and disappearance.

### UI and File Structure Gaps

  * **Incomplete UI Controls:** The "Pre-Trim" screen is missing key functionalities, such as a **Back button**, a **"Change Video"** option, and a button to **"Use Original"** video without trimming.

## **Spec-Kit: Add Move Flow Refactor (Project Phoenix)**

### **1. Problem Statement & Diagnosis**

Analysis of the application logs reveals a core architectural flaw identified as **State Object Churn**. Expensive objects (video players, state managers, health monitors) are being repeatedly created and destroyed during view transitions instead of being preserved and reused.

This core issue manifests in several critical symptoms:
* **Chronic View Re-computation:** SwiftUI views, such as `TrimmerViewWrapper`, are re-evaluated over 15 times in rapid succession, causing significant performance degradation.
* **Player Lifecycle Mismanagement:** The `AVPlayer` instance is not persistent. It's destroyed and recreated between the `previewing` and `trimming` states, leading to inefficiency and potential memory leaks.
* **Dual State Management:** The application currently uses two conflicting state management systems (`AddMoveState` enum and `AddMoveAppState` observable class), preventing a single source of truth and increasing complexity.
* **Redundant Monitoring:** The `VideoHealthMonitor` is unnecessarily stopped and restarted during state transitions, adding processing overhead.

---
### **2. Target Architecture: State-First Model**

To resolve these issues, we will implement a **State-First Architecture**. This model centralizes state and decouples it from the view lifecycle.



* **Single Source of Truth:** We will create a single `@MainActor @ObservableObject` class, `AddMoveUnifiedState`, to manage the entire flow's state. It will hold the current step (`flowState`), the video asset, player state, and references to persistent services.
* **Persistent Object Management:** The `UnifiedPlayerManager` and `VideoHealthMonitor` will be instantiated once and managed by the unified state object. Their lifecycles will be tied to the "Add Move" flow, not individual views. The `UnifiedPlayerManager` will **own** the `AVPlayer` instance.
* **State-Driven Views:** SwiftUI views will become "dumb." They will not create or own state. Instead, they will receive the `AddMoveUnifiedState` as an `@ObservedObject` and render based on its properties.
